# FERMATA syllabus (order numbers = station position on the subject line)

Line order on every subject: L1 lessons = orders 1 to 5, Deep Dive 1 = order 6, L2 lessons = orders 7 to 10, L3 lessons = orders 11 to 13, Deep Dive 2 = order 14.
Lesson ids: L1 -> `<prefix>-1-01` .. `-1-05`; L2 -> `<prefix>-2-01` .. `-2-04`; L3 -> `<prefix>-3-01` .. `-3-03`; deep -> `<prefix>-d1`, `<prefix>-d2`.

---------------------------------------------------------------
## finance (prefix fin): "Money & Finance"
L1 101
1. What money really is: medium of exchange, store of value, unit of account; why inflation slowly eats cash.
2. Income, expenses and the budget: cash flow in/out, 50/30/20 rule, paying yourself first.
3. Compound interest: interest on interest, Rule of 72, why starting early beats investing more later.
4. Debt: good, bad and expensive: APR, minimum-payment trap on credit cards, avalanche vs snowball payoff.
5. The emergency fund: 3 to 6 months of expenses, where to keep it (high-yield savings), insurance as a safety net.
D1 (30 min) "Your Money System": build a complete personal finance plan around a running case (a young professional in NYC): net worth statement, monthly cash flow, budget, debt plan, emergency fund, automation, goals with dates.
L2 201
6. Time value of money: present value and future value, discounting, simple formulas with worked numbers.
7. Inflation and real returns: nominal vs real, CPI, how inflation hits savers vs borrowers.
8. How lending and credit scores work (US): FICO factors, utilization, how banks price risk, fixed vs variable rates, mortgages basics.
9. Taxes for individuals (US): marginal vs effective rate, deductions vs credits, pre-tax vs post-tax money (illustrative numbers only).
L3 301
10. NPV and IRR: how companies decide which projects to fund.
11. Cost of capital and WACC: what investors and lenders expect, debt is cheaper than equity and why.
12. Financial ratios that tell a story: liquidity, leverage, profitability, efficiency; DuPont ROE breakdown.
D2 (55 to 60 min) "Corporate Finance Masterclass: how value is created": a running case of a mid-size company deciding on an expansion: forecast cash flows, WACC, NPV/IRR, sensitivity analysis, capital structure, dividends vs buybacks, how the decision shows up in the statements.

## accounting (prefix acc): "Accounting"
L1 101
1. Accounting: the language of business: who uses financial info and why; GAAP vs IFRS in one paragraph.
2. The accounting equation: Assets = Liabilities + Equity, always balanced, examples of transactions.
3. Debits and credits without fear: the T-account, DEALER (Dividends, Expenses, Assets increase with debits; Liabilities, Equity, Revenue with credits).
4. The three financial statements: income statement, balance sheet, cash flow statement and how they connect.
5. Cash vs accrual: when revenue and expenses are recorded, why accrual tells the truth.
D1 (30 min) "From transaction to financial statements": run a small business (a coffee cart) through one month: journal entries, ledger, trial balance, adjusting entries, statements.
L2 201
6. Revenue recognition: ASC 606 five-step model with simple examples (subscriptions, bundles).
7. Depreciation and amortization: straight-line vs declining balance, useful life, salvage value.
8. Inventory: FIFO, LIFO, weighted average and their effect on profit and taxes.
9. Adjusting entries and the month-end close: accruals, deferrals, prepaid, reconciliations, a close checklist.
L3 301
10. The cash flow statement, indirect method: from net income to cash, working-capital changes.
11. Deferred taxes demystified (ASC 740): temporary vs permanent differences, DTA/DTL with one clean example.
12. Consolidation and intercompany basics: parent and subsidiary, eliminations, US subsidiaries of foreign groups, transfer pricing in one paragraph.
D2 (55 to 60 min) "Read a 10-K like an accountant": structure of a 10-K, MD&A, notes that matter (revenue, leases, debt, taxes, segments), red flags (channel stuffing, aggressive capitalization, cash flow vs earnings gaps), quality-of-earnings checks, a walkthrough on a fictional company.

## investing (prefix inv): "Investing"
L1 101
1. Saving vs investing: when money should sit still vs work, time horizon.
2. Risk and return: the core trade-off, volatility explained simply, why higher expected return needs more risk.
3. The asset classes: stocks, bonds, cash, real estate, commodities, crypto (as speculative), what each does in a portfolio.
4. Diversification: why many uncorrelated bets reduce risk, the only "free lunch".
5. Funds: mutual funds, index funds and ETFs, active vs passive.
D1 (30 min) "Build your first portfolio": goals, horizon, risk tolerance questionnaire, simple 3-fund style allocations by age, choosing low-cost funds (no tickers recommended), automation, what to do when markets fall.
L2 201
6. Bonds: price vs yield seesaw, credit risk, duration intuition, Treasuries vs corporates.
7. Asset allocation and rebalancing: stock/bond mix, glide paths, rebalancing bands.
8. Costs and fees: expense ratios, advisory fees, the long-term cost of 1% with a clear chart.
9. Tax-advantaged accounts (US): 401(k), IRA vs Roth IRA, HSA, employer match; brief note on Italian/European equivalents (no year-specific limits).
L3 301
10. Modern Portfolio Theory: efficient frontier, correlation, Sharpe ratio.
11. Behavioral finance: loss aversion, overconfidence, herding, recency bias, how to protect yourself.
12. Factor investing: value, size, momentum, quality, low volatility, evidence and limits.
D2 (55 to 60 min) "How the economy moves markets": central banks and interest rates, inflation, the business cycle, yield curve, earnings cycle, how each asset class behaves in each phase, reading economic data releases (CPI, jobs, GDP), a framework to read the morning news.

## stocks (prefix stk): "Stock Investing"
L1 101
1. What a share really is: ownership, claim on future profits, shareholders vs company.
2. How the stock market works: exchanges, brokers, bid/ask, market vs limit orders, trading hours.
3. Reading a stock quote: price, market cap, volume, 52-week range, P/E, dividend yield, EPS.
4. Why stock prices move: earnings, expectations, interest rates, sentiment; short-term noise vs long-term value.
5. Dividends and total return: yield, payout ratio, reinvesting, price return vs total return.
D1 (35 min, use "minutes":30) "Analyze your first company": step-by-step on a fictional company: business model, revenue drivers, margins, balance sheet health, valuation snapshot, writing a one-page note.
L2 201
6. Valuation multiples: P/E, PEG, P/S, EV/EBITDA, when each is useful, comparing to peers.
7. Reading an earnings report: revenue, EPS vs estimates, guidance, the conference call, why stocks fall on "good" results.
8. Moats: competitive advantages (brand, network effects, switching costs, cost advantages, scale).
9. Growth vs value stocks: characteristics, how they behave with rates, GARP.
L3 301
10. Discounted cash flow (DCF) made simple: free cash flow, discount rate, terminal value, margin of safety.
11. Technical analysis: what it can and cannot do: trends, support/resistance, moving averages, volume; skeptical but fair.
12. Position sizing and risk management: max position size, stop-losses, diversification across positions, the math of losses (−50% needs +100%).
D2 (55 to 60 min) "Build a full stock thesis": a fictional company from scratch: industry, moat, 5-year model, DCF, multiples cross-check, bull/base/bear scenarios, risks, position size, a watch-list with what would change your mind; plus a pre-buy checklist.

## ai (prefix ai): "Using AI Well"
L1 101
1. What AI really is: large language models predict the next word, trained on huge text, what that means in practice.
2. Prompting basics: context + task + format; a weak prompt vs a strong prompt.
3. What AI is good and bad at: drafting, summarizing, explaining vs exact math, fresh facts, hallucinations.
4. Privacy and safety: what not to paste (client data, IDs, passwords), firm policies, anonymizing.
5. Everyday uses: email drafts, summaries, learning, planning, translation.
D1 (30 min) "The prompting workshop": a hands-on session improving 6 real prompts from a professional's week (an email to a client, summarizing a long PDF, explaining a tax concept, a spreadsheet formula, planning a trip, a hard conversation), with before/after chat figures.
L2 201
6. Advanced prompting: give examples, ask for a plan first, roles, constraints, iterate, ask it to critique itself.
7. AI for analysis: working with spreadsheets and data, formulas, verifying numbers, never trusting totals blindly.
8. Learning anything faster with AI: Socratic tutor mode, quizzes, explain like I'm new, spaced practice.
9. Context is everything: projects, files, memory and instructions, giving AI the right background.
L3 301
10. Agents and automation: AI that takes actions, tools, when to let it act and where to keep a human check.
11. Building with AI: no-code tools vs APIs, prototyping small apps, what a non-programmer can build today.
12. Evaluating AI output like a professional: a verification checklist, sources, bias, accountability stays with you.
D2 (55 to 60 min) "Design your AI-powered workflow": map a real week of work (an accountant: reconciliations, client emails, memos, research), find tasks to automate, design prompts and templates, review steps, risk controls, measure time saved, build a personal prompt library.

## self (prefix self): "Self Improvement"
L1 101
1. Habits: cue, routine, reward; habits are votes for who you want to be.
2. The 1% rule: small daily improvements compound (link to compound interest).
3. Goals vs systems: SMART goals, why systems beat goals, implementation intentions ("when X, I will Y").
4. Attention and deep work: multitasking myth, focus blocks, phone management.
5. Energy foundations: sleep, movement, light, hydration as performance tools (non-medical).
D1 (30 min) "Design your personal operating system": morning and evening routines, weekly review, habit tracker, environment design, a written plan the reader fills in.
L2 201
6. Learning how to learn: spaced repetition, retrieval practice, interleaving, the forgetting curve.
7. Better decisions: pre-mortem, 10/10/10, reversible vs irreversible decisions, avoiding decision fatigue.
8. Communication: active listening, clear writing (BLUF), giving and receiving feedback.
9. Productivity systems: Eisenhower matrix, time blocking, the weekly review, saying no.
L3 301
10. Mental models: first principles, inversion, second-order thinking, circle of competence.
11. Negotiation basics: BATNA, interests vs positions, anchoring, win-win.
12. Leading people: delegation, coaching juniors, one-on-ones, building trust.
D2 (55 to 60 min) "Your 90-day transformation plan": vision, 3 goals, lead vs lag measures, weekly scorecards, obstacles and if-then plans, accountability, review rituals; a full worked example and a template.

## inner (prefix inner): "Inner Growth"
L1 101
1. What inner growth means: self-awareness, the observer, why it matters for a busy professional.
2. Breath and presence: a 3-minute practice you can do on the train (use a breath figure).
3. Naming emotions: emotional granularity, "name it to tame it", a feelings vocabulary.
4. The inner critic and self-compassion: kindness, common humanity, mindfulness.
5. Gratitude and attention to the good: negativity bias, three good things.
D1 (30 min) "Who am I becoming? A guided reflection": a structured journaling session with prompts across values, strengths, relationships, regrets, hopes, and a closing practice.
L2 201
6. Stoicism: the dichotomy of control, negative visualization, the view from above.
7. Meditation styles explained: focused attention, open monitoring, loving-kindness, body scan, walking.
8. Values: your inner compass, choosing top values, living by them under pressure.
9. Journaling methods: morning pages, gratitude, worry dump, evening review, prompts.
L3 301
10. Ego, identity and letting go: stories we tell, attachment, non-reactivity.
11. Resilience: growing through hard times, reframing, support networks, when to ask for professional help.
12. Meaning and purpose: ikigai, contribution, flow, legacy.
D2 (55 to 60 min) "Philosophies of inner peace": Stoics, Buddhist mindfulness, yoga and breath traditions, modern psychology (ACT, positive psychology), what they agree on, and a personal daily practice built from all of them; with guided practices inside.
